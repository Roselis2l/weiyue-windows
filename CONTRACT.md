Canonical message: `{id:string,groupId:string,senderId:string,senderName:string,text:string,time:number}` (epoch ms).

All IDs string; unsafe numeric imported IDs rejected. Message time range `[start,end)`.

## Continuous synchronization (1.1.22)

Latest synchronization preserves durable `latestPendingRanges`, with `latestPending` as a compatibility view. It rotates selected groups, interleaves head reads, and persists each successful page before continuing. Request size remains 20; automatic batches use at most 8 pages with an 8-second soft time budget, manual batches 120 pages/90 seconds, with at least one second between page requests. An in-flight request can exceed the soft budget. Individual requests retain a 12-second timeout, while each automatic run has a separate 20-second abort deadline covering identity verification and pagination. Cursor progress uses decimal message IDs and never treats local duplicates or a premature empty page as proof that a gap is closed. Historical fetching remains separate and starts before the oldest local message.

A successful automatic cycle schedules its next check five seconds after completion. The current reading group is eligible first; other selected groups become eligible for a head read at least 60 seconds after the previous completed head. This is a local engineering interval, not an official service guarantee. Head and gap jobs share one bounded queue, preserving fair background and gap progress; shortening the idle delay must not create parallel work or treat duplicate pages as new messages. The renderer polls local IPC state every two seconds without issuing network reads. No additional automatic contacts polling is introduced.

Background-head and gap turns persist across automatic batches, including batches that complete only one slow page. After at most four successful requests other than the current group's head (including background heads), insert a current-group head check without losing the saved background/gap turns. The five-second delay is an idle-success completion lower bound; the 20-second deadline cancels in-flight work for an entire automatic run, not a foreground or all-group end-to-end freshness guarantee.

An unresolved gap may be probed once initially, then its probes use a separate minimum 60-second cooldown. Frequent foreground head completions must neither reset nor indefinitely defer that gap's retry clock. Multiple unresolved ranges within one group rotate, and normal pending-range progress must not postpone their independent retry eligibility. Normal pending ranges that are still progressing remain eligible for short-batch work independently of the background head interval. `retryUnresolvedGroupIds` gates only unresolved probes, never discards saved ranges or turns a premature empty page into completion.

The captured account `AutoSync` policy guards synchronization and legacy content enrichment. Network/service failure backoff still starts at 60 seconds and caps at 15 minutes independently of the five-second success interval. A 429 response stops the batch, retains at least 60 seconds of cooldown and honors longer Retry-After values even across pause/resume; authentication, permission, risk-control and ignored-cursor failures pause requests for explicit user recovery. Account switching must not attribute an old request's failure to the new account. Synchronization does not advance reading high-water marks.

`scripts/lib/offline-merge.cjs` merges isolated account states by group and stable message ID, retains destination settings and reading marks, adds only recovered messages beyond the prior read boundary to unread IDs, and archives conflicting variants. Historical backups are coverage evidence; backup-only saved items are not implicitly resurrected. Installation data and private migration archives must never enter source or portable distributions.

## Reading filters and navigation (1.1.14)

`messages.query` additionally accepts `senderIds:string[]`: omitted/empty means all senders, nonempty is an exact normalized-ID OR set; a simultaneously supplied legacy `senderId` intersects this set. All other filters intersect it. `messages.locate` uses the same exported `compareMessages` ordering as the query.

The reading UI accepts strict minute-resolution China wall times. `WeiyueFilters.parseChinaMinuteRange(start,end)` maps start to UTC+08:00 minute start and end to the next minute exclusive, rejecting inverted selected minutes before conversion. The core retains `[start,end)` epoch milliseconds; summary time semantics are unchanged.

`readStates[groupId].readThroughId` is the persisted monotonic high-water mark, separate from `anchorId`/`anchorOffset`. The new latest-read button loads a window around this exact ID using `messages.locate`, clears obstructing filters and writes only an anchor. It never sends `readThroughId` to `read.set`. Only validated native reading intent or the distinct explicit “加载到最新” action can advance the mark. No read-state schema migration is required.

The member picker preserves its search input node and selection set during queries, list updates and IME composition. Selected IDs, not names or visible rows, determine the query.

## Compact message controls and emphasis (1.1.18)

Message selection, bookmark and important-member controls are visually hidden and pointer-inert by default while retaining their geometry and keyboard tab order. Pointer reveal starts only when entering the avatar; the avatar, checkbox and icon buttons share a contiguous region until the pointer leaves it. Hovering the message body must not reveal controls. Keyboard `:focus-visible` keeps the focused region visible without leaving controls exposed after an ordinary mouse click. Selected rows retain their chosen appearance and batch count even when their controls are hidden.

Ordinary member content uses a white rounded message bubble on a pale gray conversation surface, with a small upper-left tail and a light shadow. Bubble width follows its content up to a bounded maximum; short text does not fill the row. Long text wraps, and media/reference/attachment sizing must fit the available bubble width without collapsing images or overflowing at the minimum supported window size. Avatars and member information remain outside the bubble.

Important-member names remain bold in their configured color, while their content bubble uses its configured color. The new default is a pale pink important bubble with dark red nickname and body text, with no copied decorations or gradients. There is no important-member left stripe. Explicit selection and reading-location highlights retain separate outer markers. Bookmark message excerpts use the same bubble appearance. Identity matching remains based on stable sender IDs. Verified system notifications remain compact expandable lines rather than ordinary bubbles.

Left-button dragging begins on non-interactive whitespace outside the bubble after a movement threshold. The full-width message row and copy region retain that empty drag surface even when the content bubble is short. Bubble content retains native selection; bubble padding, buttons, links, images and inputs cannot start dragging. Release, pointer leaving the window, focus loss, view replacement and cancellation terminate it, with the trailing click suppressed. Only actual user-driven reading movement may update read state; filtered views and historical revisits cannot reduce its high-water mark.

## Account appearance (1.1.21)

`settings.appearance` contains six independent fields: `chatBackground`, `memberName`, `importantName`, `memberBubble`, `importantBubble`, and `importantText`. Their new defaults are `#f1f3f2`, `#52634b`, `#860000`, `#ffffff`, `#fcd3d4`, and `#860000`. Only strict three- or six-digit HEX inputs are accepted and canonicalized to six digits; `importantText` additionally accepts `null` for automatic readable text. Arbitrary CSS expressions, alpha values, unknown keys and malformed objects are rejected by the main process. The shared renderer/CommonJS color module is authoritative for defaults, normalization and contrast calculation. An unconfigured appearance uses the six new defaults. An existing stored appearance missing the new text field preserves its original five valid colors and uses automatic important text; reading never writes a migration or overrides saved customization. Explicit saves require all six fields.

The separate `settings.appearance.save` route binds the expected account, validates the complete appearance value and updates only that account's appearance settings. It does not touch credentials, messages, read state, saved items or export associations. Applying saved CSS variables does not rebuild message DOM or advance a reading revision. A stale dialog cannot save to another account.

Appearance editing uses a local synthetic preview. Color pickers and validated HEX inputs edit a draft; cancel or close leaves saved state and the real conversation untouched. Restoring defaults only changes that draft until Save succeeds. Nickname and explicitly chosen important-body contrast warnings do not silently alter the user's chosen colors. Ordinary body text, automatic important text and links select suitable colors with at least 4.5:1 contrast; an explicitly chosen important-body color remains exact even if it is low contrast. Neutral nested reference and attachment cards remain independently legible. Appearance affects conversation and bookmark messages, not unrelated application panels.

Compact bubbles reduce padding and minimum dimensions without reducing the message font size, media aspect ratios, maximum text width or avatar-control hit areas. Unused space outside the content bubble remains draggable; body selection and interactive content retain their native behavior. Forced-color accessibility settings continue to override decorative colors as appropriate.

Confirmed system notices use compact small text directly on the conversation surface, with no bubble background, border, shadow or tail, including expanded notice text and saved excerpts. Their text contrasts with the selected conversation background and does not inherit either member bubble color. Long notices remain expandable. Stable server metadata, not sender display names, determines the system-notice exception.

## Bidirectional local paging (1.1.23)

The reading view prepends the previous local page at the top or appends the next at the bottom after actual directional user intent. The edge tolerance is two pixels. Accepted wheel bursts, blank-area drag gestures and PageUp/PageDown/ArrowUp/ArrowDown key holds request at most one page each, under one in-flight lock even across context changes. Keyboard handling is restricted to the reading region's safe focus or pointer context and excludes editable controls, controls with their own keyboard behavior, media, modal dialogs and other panes. Rendering, media layout changes, appearance previews and initial navigation cannot create paging intent. Paging never invokes network synchronization or the separate load-latest action.

Prepending and appending preserve existing message nodes, text selection and the visible message's pixel offset. Prior read intent is invalidated so newly inserted, unseen messages do not become read merely through pagination; loading earlier messages never moves the read high-water mark backwards. Account, group or filter changes invalidate pending responses. Manual controls remain available, errors are visible and retryable, and local beginning/end boundaries stop loading. Blank-area dragging finishes before its single page is inserted, so a held gesture keeps control of the viewport.

The existing local `messages.query` operation accepts optional, mutually exclusive `startId`, `beforeId` or `afterId` string anchors within the complete current filtered group. `beforeId` returns at most `limit` messages immediately before the anchor, in ascending order, excludes the anchor and reports the exact offset and full filtered total. Missing or invalid anchors are rejected. The renderer validates continuity before applying the page. Background refresh keeps the expanded window using bounded local query segments with consistent revisions; it does not silently shrink the loaded result to the original page. Ordinary offset queries and manual navigation retain their behavior.

## Local summary deletion (1.1.15)

`summary.delete.preview` accepts stable `{accountId,summaryId,groupId}` strings and returns the selected title plus a random, short-lived `confirmationToken`. The application displays an HTML confirmation dialog with cancellation as the default. A confirmed `summary.delete` request includes the same IDs, token and `confirmed:true`. The main process consumes the token once and checks its account epoch, group, target ID, expiry and saved target fingerprint; a stale target or changed account is rejected. The preview store is bounded to 32 entries with a five-minute TTL; the token never carries credentials.

`Store.removeSummary(accountId,summaryId,groupId)` removes only the exact matching summary and persists atomically. Cancellation and failed persistence keep the summary and revision unchanged. The renderer removes the card only after a successful response; identity does not depend on title or array position. Restart reads the persisted result.

Deletion changes only `summaries`. Chat messages, bookmarks, reading state, other accounts, export journals and existing Notion pages remain unchanged. There is no network request or remote deletion.

## Media contracts (1.1.12)

`fetchImage({url,fetch,signal,allowHeif:false,allowRasterMimeCorrection:false})` retains JPEG/PNG/GIF/WebP MIME and signature checks by default. Chat image display alone opts into raster MIME correction when both the response declaration and actual signature are supported raster types; the verified signature selects the MIME without rewriting original bytes. Missing/unknown declarations, HTML and SVG remain rejected. Image display also explicitly opts into `allowHeif:true`; only strict HEVC-specific HEIF brands with a supported declared MIME return `{bytes,mime:'image/heic'}`. Generic BMFF or an extension is insufficient. Avatar and AI callers do not inherit either opt-in.

The HEIC decoder runs locally in a separate sandbox and terminable worker. Input is at most 20 MiB; display PNG is at most 64 MiB. Pixel/dimension and time budgets are enforced by the decoder. The image viewer receives `{bytes:displayPng,mime:'image/png',original:{bytes:heic,mime:'image/heic'}}`, displays PNG and saves the unchanged original with `.heic` / `.heif`. Ordinary image contracts remain unchanged. Cache accounting includes both buffers and is account scoped.

PDF/video attachment descriptors must originate in explicit, validated message metadata. Fetches use the current authorized account and a restricted media proxy, never renderer-supplied arbitrary URLs. PDF is limited to 50 MiB and rendered to canvas using the bundled PDF.js without document scripting. Videos are limited to 200 MiB and supported MP4/WebM containers; playback depends on Electron codec support and exposes native controls. Opening a video preview is the user action that starts playback; message-list media never starts automatically. A dedicated viewer can save validated original files through the native file dialog. Account changes invalidate outstanding viewers and reads.

Message HTTP/HTTPS links are text-derived display links, not application instructions. The renderer uses safe text nodes and a restricted external-open bridge; clicking a validated link opens the system default browser without an additional confirmation dialog. Media display does not expand AI/Notion consent or content scope. Reading anchors, unread state and Chinese composition behavior must remain unchanged while media loads.

Release source and portable packages include the reference MIT notice, runtime notices, libheif/libde265 corresponding source and rebuild materials, and PDF.js/font licenses. Private diagnostics, real messages, account files, credentials and installation backups are excluded. Test counts and real-target acceptance must be recorded only after execution.

## Reversible local message recycling (1.1.17)

`messages.cleanup.info/preview/apply` bind account ID and group ID. The preview takes strict China minute strings (`startMinute`, inclusive `endMinute`), optional stable `retainSenderIds`, and `retainSummaryRanges`. Main-process confirmation tokens are random, single use, expire after five minutes and bind the account epoch, exact selection and store fingerprint. A stale decision cannot delete a newly protected record. Apply is blocked during synchronization, enrichment, AI or Notion writes. Success alone changes UI/revision and clears prepared AI content.

Messages remain in the account JSON with validated `localDeletion` metadata and a recoverable `messageTrash` batch. Normal queries, locate, statistics, media access and new summaries exclude them; synchronization frontiers still include them. Import and sync cannot remove tombstones. `messages.trash.list/restore` restores a specific batch only within the captured account/group. There is no permanent purge and no claim of disk-space reclamation.

The first successful nonempty recycle changes that account's `schemaVersion` from 1 to 2. Reading, previewing or cancelling does not migrate it; restoring all batches does not downgrade it. This version reads both formats, while 1.1.16 and earlier must reject format 2 instead of silently discarding recycle markers. Downgrading requires the complete pre-recycle backup. The historical offline merge helper rejects any recycled-format input rather than attempting to merge batch identities.

Bookmarks, summary references, export associations, read anchors/high-water marks, unread IDs and live synchronization anchors are always retained. Summary intervals use their actual saved `[start,end)` provenance. Missing, contradictory or untraceable provenance conservatively protects the relevant group or account and produces a visible reason. Test data must be isolated; implementing cleanup is not authorization to delete real messages.

## References, Live Photos and archives (1.1.17)

`referenceVersion:1` and bounded `references[]` preserve actual `retweeted_status` originals and additional `url_objects[].status` posts. Stable post IDs or validated returned links identify them. Media proxy sections `reference-0` through `reference-19` resolve only stored descriptors under the active account. Plain text quotes without target IDs or URLs remain text with an explanation, never guessed by nickname/content. Legacy rich messages can be enriched once to obtain the new descriptors.

The official `live_photo_video_fid` field becomes a video attachment with `livePhoto:true`; still images use the existing image viewer and the companion video starts playback when the user opens its preview. Archive attachments support bounded ZIP/RAR/7z downloads via validated metadata, format signatures and the existing restricted transport. `attachment.download` prompts for a native save path, de-duplicates in-flight actions, invalidates on account change and never extracts or executes the file.

Opening a short validated HEVC MP4 preview automatically attempts one local compatible WebM conversion (VP8/Vorbis) when native playback cannot display video. Failure exposes explicit retry and original-file saving without a retry loop. A fresh sandboxed decoder and terminable worker have no network or account access. Input is bounded to 20 MiB, 30 seconds, 4096 pixels per edge, 12 million pixels and 1800 source frames; actual decoding also limits pixel count, frame count, time and allocations. Preview resolution is at most 1280 by 720 and output is below 64 MiB. The verified single-thread WASM has a 2 GiB linear-memory maximum; the 64 MiB allocation and output budgets are not a claim about total memory. The outer job terminates after 120 seconds. Closing the viewer or changing accounts cancels it. The opened preview also starts playback after compatible conversion, while preserving pause, volume and seek controls. Switching previews or closing one stops its prior media. Saving always uses the original unchanged MP4. Conversion failure leaves the original available to save.

The FFmpeg core retains its GPL-2.0-or-later license, dependency notices, corresponding-source archives, hashes and rebuild materials. The application's MIT notice does not relicense those components. Source provenance and platform/rebuild limitations are documented separately; source availability is not a claim that the official binary was reproduced bit for bit.

The official default chat picker snapshot adds 92 names and six explicitly confirmed file-path codes to the retained 308-name snapshot: 406 mappings, 405 unique bundled PNGs. `extensions-source.json` and `extensions-manifest.json` record source and exact resource hashes. This is the verified combined coverage, not a claim of every exclusive or animated category.
